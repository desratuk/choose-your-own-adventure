# Return to Sender — second-pass editorial review

**Current status:** revised and independently passed. The body preserves the baseline critique; implementation and acceptance are recorded at the end.

**Full spoilers.** This report discusses solutions, consequences and endings. Reviewed against `stories/return-to-sender/story.ts` and its outline at **8df3b37**, with comparison to that commit’s parent.

## Commissioning judgment

**Commissionable, distinctive, and substantially improved; request a focused developmental revision before publication.** The premise is strong enough for a family Ghostbusters adventure: an ordinary delivery becomes an investigation into people whom a city has forgotten. The protagonist contributes a profession rather than a superpower. The best resolution makes delivery, acknowledgement and attention to absent people into the same dramatic action. This is considerably more interesting than a sequence of familiar ghosts and borrowed catchphrases.

The remaining problem is not that the story needs more pages or more ways to lose. It needs to trust its dramatic situations more. The revised text repeatedly explains responsible conduct, its own supernatural accounting and the meaning of its theme. That makes the moral reasoning unusually clear, but too often gives the story the sound of an exemplary incident report. Meanwhile, the best outcome combines heavily advertised compassionate actions with a capacity requirement that important routes do not teach until after commitment. The reader receives the answer more readily than the information needed to budget for it.

Audience assumption: an independent reader of roughly **11–14 and upwards**, plus family co-reading and nostalgic adults. No official target age was supplied. Younger readers can follow the broad adventure, but the later distinctions between original service, certified copies, an open acknowledgement and a separate release file need particularly concrete staging. “Official tie-in quality” here means an editorial standard for a publishable licensed family adventure; this is not a claim of licensor approval, a canon audit or rights clearance.

Longer routes need selective compression and more dramatic variety, not a larger total word count. ## Strengths to protect

**The hook arrives through a working life.** R01’s wet socks, disproportionate cake tip and rear brake cable are efficient details. “The cake tipped better than the lawyers” gives the courier a voice before the supernatural premise appears. R02’s antique cylinder and R03’s clerk unable to produce a solid pen turn an abstract mystery into visible, playable business. The opening now explains the franchise through the job instead of stopping to list equipment and biographies.

**The original protagonist earns a place in the franchise.** Route knowledge, checked addresses, handling catches and recipient acknowledgement form one coherent competence. R08’s checked-entrance mark, H03’s reassuring exchange with Ernie and C11’s supported pencil make that competence tangible. C09 preserves the division of work: the courier delivers; the professionals operate the dangerous equipment. This is much better than merely telling the player that the famous characters are impressed.

**The central compassion is enacted at its best moments.** Hollis moving his cup from an empty place setting in R07, Slimer tapping from his chair in F03, Tull’s “I rather hoped they had” in C03, and his final “I think … I’ll walk” in C10 are restrained and effective. The completed job allows people to stop working. That is a clear emotional idea, and it belongs to this particular story.

**The alternatives now respect ordinary judgment.** E02 accepts the commissioned handover. F05 acknowledges an incomplete diagnosis without making Egon malicious or making the courier responsible for information they were never given. E05 allows apology, another known entrance or a safe handover. E06’s protected main vault and preservation of an already-delivered notice make failure proportionate. Keep all of these repairs.

**The bureaucracy has comic potential beyond references.** The CLOSED-sign fort in R05, the REQUEST FOR FORM in T03, Voss’s “Not a demonstration” in E05 and Janine’s “Two addresses … One invoice” in E10 are economical and specific. The handless clock and physical return circuit provide useful eerie imagery. An antagonist need not be a villain: an institution’s unfinished obligation is sufficient opposition here.

## Must resolve before publication

### 1. Make the best route an inference challenge with fair advance information

There are two different issues, which should not be solved by simply making everything more obscure.

First, **C07 supplies the exact late solution to every reader**: “Keep the receipt open; take named copies to the staff who are elsewhere.” It is available even without `copyRule` or `roster`. R05/T03 have already stated the copy procedure explicitly; C03 introduces the missing twelve and asks the player to “Check every name, including the absent staff.” C07 then explains that the receipt remains open and presents the completed plan. The reader’s consequential earlier act is buying the right evidence flag. They have little need to connect those facts themselves at the moment of application.

The UP/DOWN choice is better than before: C04 no longer has Tull announce an up-run beside a crowd pointing at the answer, and the stronger draught gives the wrong mouth a physical attraction. But C02 provides a clear diagram only a few scenes before C04. This is a fair observation check, not a demanding deduction. It cannot carry the full brief that the best ending should require independently connecting earlier clues.

Second, **the ten-point release reserve is not fairly distributed**. F02 explains it. The clean solo R05/lobby route never visits F02; neither does the clean researched-team route through F01 → F04 → F05 → F06. Both first learn the numerical requirement at C08, after selecting the follow-up and spending five more headroom. On Hard, a recoverable mistake can therefore turn a well-intentioned attempt to finish into E06. The meter is visible, but the relevant operating threshold was not available on those routes.

**Smallest useful remedy:** retain the early procedure and roster evidence, but shorten C07’s follow-up choice to an action such as keeping the receipt open without spelling out its whole purpose and method. Audit surrounding prose so one meaningful connection is left to the reader. Establish the controlled-release reserve through an early communication that every main route can receive; do not add a warning immediately before the decisive choice. A reader should have to remember and apply the rule, not discover it after paying the cost.

**Acceptance:** a new reader can explain a best-ending decision using two separated earlier facts; the final choice text does not provide the complete explanation. Every route offering the late attempt has previously made the relevant capacity constraint learnable. A missed clue can still produce an ordinary good ending, and existing recoveries remain available.

### 2. Replace repeated explanation with selected dramatic evidence

The rewrite has fixed genuine problems, but some corrective language remains visible as corrective language. R03 says “Nobody attacks. Nobody disappears.” F01 tells us that nobody mistakes the readings for normal business. F02 says the trap does not release obligations. E05 adds “Nobody grabs or carries you.” C06 clarifies “No one here is being captured; distance is the problem.” E08 insists that nobody calls confinement release. E09 explicitly states what the courier and team are not entitled or expected to do.

Any one of these may clarify a complicated branch. Taken together, they repeatedly interrupt the imagined scene to certify its fairness. The endings then explain the moral distinctions again. The best ending’s factual accounting is necessary, but C09, C10 and E11 all summarize completed counts; the emotional release sometimes arrives underneath an audit trail.

This affects franchise feel as well as rhythm. The current team are competent and considerate, which is right, but much of their page time consists of explaining procedures. Peter starts speeches more often than he gets an amusing line. Ray supplies a survey, reads paperwork and visibly feels the intended emotion. Egon and Winston have different jobs, but their dialogue often shares the narrator’s careful explanatory register. Janine has the most consistent voice. F06’s “before Peter can improve the arrangement” is a good comic seed; more like that can emerge from the existing task without returning to negligence or cruel jokes.

**Smallest useful remedy:** make a line-level pass across R03, F01–F06, C06 and E07–E11. Keep each essential rule at its first necessary explanation and in conditional aftermath where the reader needs reassurance; remove adjacent restatements that merely announce correct conduct. Spend a few reclaimed lines on a ghost’s surprising action, a physical complication or an actual exchange among the team. In C09, dramatize one acknowledgement before efficiently accounting for the other eleven.

**Acceptance:** the reader can still explain what the tag, trap and grid each do, and no captured person vanishes from the accounting. But a typical scene is remembered for something someone did or said, rather than for a qualification about what did not happen. Peter, Ray, Egon and Winston are distinguishable without relying only on name tags or assigned machinery.

### 3. Align ending labels with the story’s moral contract

E01 remains labelled **bad** after R01 explicitly offers “You can say no.” Dolores accepts the refusal, Janine collects the parcel, and the immediate trouble is contained. The remaining loss is the protagonist’s unplayed mystery. Calling that bad retains a small but conspicuous version of the very punishment the rewrite has removed from the prose. The cleanest remedy is to classify it as a modest good ending, with the unresolved mystery intact. Do not invent harm to justify the label.

E03 deserves a more delicate adjustment. The courier really does close an unsigned job prematurely, so a failure label has a defensible professional meaning. But on the team route Winston offers custody inside the ending, and the narration makes the player decline it without another choice. The team then delivers the notice locally, leaving the same broad release work as ordinary good endings E09/E10. The reader has chosen a shortcut; the ending also chooses how stubborn they are. Its closing reprimand is longer than necessary.

E04 and the containment successes also approach one another in practical outcome: everyone is safe, clerks remain confined, and release becomes future work. E04’s distinction is accepting the wrong service and timetable, whereas E02/E07 assign responsibility deliberately. Make that difference concrete rather than relying on the moralizing sentence “You chose its timetable.”

**Smallest useful remedy:** relabel E01; let E03 judge the unsigned delivery already chosen without inventing a further refusal or extended character indictment; give E04 one concrete consequence of delayed tracing that distinguishes it from a properly owned release investigation. Keep failures non-graphic and proportionate.

**Acceptance:** readers can state why each bad outcome differs from a good one in terms of their chosen action and its consequence. Declining an optional hazardous job is not presented as misconduct. An ending does not manufacture an additional unchosen mistake to secure its classification.

## High-value refinements

### The protagonist needs a small change, not a new biography

R01 establishes both practical need and sensitivity to being treated as “a pair of legs with a bag.” E11 confirms expertise, but the courier is already unusually perceptive, respectful and patient before most choices. Their best-route arc is predominantly other people learning to appreciate an already-complete person.

There is a subtler arc available: from proving a delivery was completed to understanding whom completion serves. R08 briefly realizes it when the courier does not hurry the ghost away from the empty stool. E10 articulates it, somewhat too explicitly. Let one early moment show understandable impatience or a wish to finish a paid round, then let C11 or C09 visibly change how the courier works. No tragic history or named identity is needed. A quiet payoff to the brake-cable need would also make the final return to normal life feel personally earned; the current van-registration coda demonstrates competence but does not resolve that opening desire.

Tull likewise has an affecting hint of interior life—the hoped-for departure of his searchers and the remembered train—but the other forty-six people are mostly totals. Give one existing clerk a recurring gesture or small remembered preference, usable within the existing group illustrations. Paying off that detail among the twelve at C09 would make “everybody” more than correct arithmetic. The obstacle’s motivation is clear; its emotional scale needs one human-sized anchor.

R06 does not need a new subplot. Its existing inspection plan could justify a distinct access advantage or a brief conditional payoff using the already-recorded visit. Alternatively, retain it openly as a less efficient but characterful source of an ordinary-good route. Do not describe the catch sketch as a meaningful interactive advantage when later text supplies that knowledge unconditionally.

R08’s alternative to “finish his thought” is simply taking the address; the continuation costs nothing, so attentive reading is universally preferable there. That is acceptable as a conversational beat. Not every pair of buttons needs a trap or resource penalty.

### Convergence and replay need distinct experiences, not more endings

The solo lobby, service stair, tunnel and team approaches feel different on the way in. Trap cargo and companions survive convergence in the prose, and the best ending is available through several strategies. Those are genuine strengths.

After C01, however, the essential dramatic sequence is shared. C09–C10 then run without another substantive decision, which makes sense after success has been earned, but reduces replay discovery. Six good endings contain fewer than six radically different resolutions: E02/E07/E08 are different routes to owned confinement, E09/E10 are local delivery with remaining work, and E11 is complete release. That is a sufficient outcome range, provided the presentation does not imply ten equally extensive alternate stories.

The best inexpensive replay improvement is one or two route-specific reactions within the common climax. A team-assisted run should offer a little more ensemble pleasure; a records/tunnel run can pay off a detail seen below the city. Preserve convergence and avoid new branches merely to inflate the map. Trim R04’s repeated arrival description on repeat visits if rereading the hub proves tiring.

### Urgency should be felt as well as counted

The rising grid load is now coherent: the tag reports strain, reserve cooling slows it, and a failed dispatch aggravates it. F04 earns its long wait with active work and cooling. The 11:20 bell gives the night a useful appointment rather than a generic countdown.

Emotionally, however, capable containment is available early and all terminal failures restore public safety. The remaining urgency is the clerks’ chance to go home tonight. That can be enough, but repeated assurances sometimes make it feel as though the only loss is additional paperwork. Strengthen the cost of one more night through Tull or a recurring clerk, and use a few bodily or environmental changes as the load rises. This does not require physical danger or an irresponsible team. A ghost repeating a futile ordinary action can be more disturbing than another numerical explanation.

The system also charges small visits and a long cooled wait in the same five-point units. That is acceptable abstraction, not elapsed-minute simulation. Keep language consistent with strain and opportunity, and avoid suggesting that every unit corresponds to the same duration. Before publication, observe whether fresh readers understand the visible rising percentage versus the spoken “ten points of spare capacity.”

## Previous revision: retain the repairs, complete the editorial pass

The parent version made obeying the original commission a disastrous bad ending, let the tag automatically capture nearby ghosts, punished sensible investigations without useful information, treated Slimer as access equipment, and allowed a nominally best resolution without fully serving the captives. It also advertised the correct tube through dialogue and staging and removed the courier from the team climax.

The current version addresses those problems materially. R05 and T03 now earn their detours; F03 has become a relationship; the courier performs the final deliveries; E05 provides recovery; and the 47-person accounting makes best genuinely different from local success. These are structural improvements, not cosmetic fixes. Their cost is extra procedure, caution and explanation. The next pass should make those sound story principles less conspicuous on the surface, not restore the earlier unfairness to manufacture excitement.

## Ranked revision brief

1. **Fair challenge — C07/C08, with early R03/F01/F06 information:** remove the supplied complete solution from the late choice and make the release reserve available on every main approach. Success requires remembered evidence; failure never depends on a previously undisclosed operational threshold.
2. **Dramatic compression — R03, F01–F06, C06, C09–C10, E07–E11:** reduce repeated explanatory and corrective statements, keeping all essential capture accounting. Reinvest selected lines in action, distinct voices and one personally recognizable acknowledgement.
3. **Ending fairness — E01, E03, E04:** align labels and consequences with the voluntary commission; eliminate an unchosen refusal in E03; distinguish delayed tracing from responsible containment concretely.
4. **Emotional arc — R01/R08/C11/C09/E11:** connect the courier’s opening impatience or material need to a changed act of care and a modest personal payoff. Give at least one absent clerk memorable individuality without adding a separate subplot.
5. **Detour and replay payoff — R06/C01, common climax:** make one distinctive investigation detail matter later and add a small route-specific reward for returning readers. Do not add length, endings or terminal pitfalls simply to raise their count.

After these changes, a small cold-reader session should answer four questions the source cannot: whether the late solution feels discovered; whether the meter’s reserve rule was remembered; where procedural reading starts to feel repetitive; and whether readers can recall a clerk as a person rather than only the number forty-seven. The story already has the concept, humane stakes and structural range. Publication readiness depends on making that experience felt with the same precision with which the current draft accounts for it.
