# The Stopped Clocks — story bible

## Premise and editorial intent

On 12 November 1985, a century-old letter puts an unnamed, gender-neutral sixteen-year-old Preservation Society volunteer beside Doc Brown's arriving time train. At 10:04 PM Hill Valley freezes. A future museum owner, Rex Tannen, is using a chrono-clamp on the clock tower to turn the town into a permanent commercial exhibit.

The player helps Doc build a stabilizer from a 1985 regulator, an unused 1885 clock wheel and a 2085 power cell. The central conflict is preservation for the people who live somewhere versus preservation that prevents them living. The player supplies mechanical patience, local knowledge and the decisive action at the clock; Doc supplies time-travel expertise.

The familiar letter/train/clock framework supports a new museum investigation, not a film recap. Ordinary success saves the town. The best route also exposes and catches Rex by connecting separately acquired evidence and knowledge. A hurried success leaves the town with a permanent missing minute.

## Fixed continuity

- Present: Tuesday 12 November 1985. The 1955 lightning strike stopped the clock at 10:04. Knowing that exact second enabled Doc and Marty to send Marty home from 1955. Marty is frozen offstage in 1985.
- Doc has a flying time train, not a DeLorean. His family is safe at home; its date/location is unspecified.
- The letter is dated 1885. Doc writes it after every successful outcome and leaves it with Western Union. Its P.S. is **“Leave the past something to work with.”** It is a thematic clue, not an explicit spare-wheel instruction.
- The player owns a Walkman and a personally recorded summer cassette. On the shop route they trade the machine but keep the tape.
- Only after S04 does the player wear a chrono-field bracelet. It protects them and carried objects. Frozen people must not be touched.
- S09 establishes that below twenty remaining minutes the clamp begins crushing its held interval; zero makes the freeze permanent. The missing-minute outcome is a consequence of this early rule, not a newly disclosed deadline.
- The 1885 visit begins on 28 August, before the new courthouse clock is unveiled. Doc's younger self is the blacksmith; meeting him changes history.
- Doc offers a concealed back-lane escort, not permission to meet himself. The player may instead choose to approach the blacksmith despite that separation.
- Taking the installed clock wheel is detected immediately on returning to Doc in S25. Doc insists on repair; he never knowingly installs it. Repair occurs in 1885 before departing. No original-wheel arrival at the finale exists.
- The replacement left behind after taking the mounted wheel is usable: the town can fit and regulate it. The danger is changing the clock's established calibration/maintenance history and the recorded lightning time Marty relied on. The fading train confirms that this alteration has disrupted that history; E05 describes a working replacement clock with a different recorded strike time. Never claim that the past lacks capable clockmakers or that the replacement cannot be found. The train can use a freshly calibrated spare because its regulator needs a new reference, not preservation of an existing time record.
- The 2085 museum contains a scan of the real frozen town, including the possible future failure of the player and Doc. It is not the actual 1985 square physically moved to 2085.
- Rex falsely claims the residents donated their moments. His public live interview channel is demonstrated in S31. This fact has a concrete climax payoff.
- The Authority gets a warrant from the stolen jump log but cannot pinpoint Rex inside his private field from the log alone. Its explanation establishes the usefulness of a public connection reaching its century without prescribing an interview. The player must connect that property with Rex's customer channel from S31.
- Rex demonstrates that he can end his customer appearances from his wrist. At S44 he initially refuses the interview, then accepts a paid-attraction opportunity he believes he can control. The player uses his one permitted question to ask for the residents' contracts.
- Rex's cuffed arrest, not simply having alerted the Authority, controls arrest-specific aftermath. Use `rexCaught` for those distinctions.
- `rexExposed` records the actual S44 broadcast, independently of arrest; visiting the museum alone does not expose Rex. E09/E11 acknowledge exposure without inventing an arrest, and E11 also acknowledges a successful arrest.
- On every successful route, Red answers the volunteer and agrees that the tower's bells should remain silent at night. The best ending carries this agreement onto the keeper's clipboard.
- A static illustration must match every arrival state: notably S41 depicts assembly before success/failure diverges; S44 depicts the broadcast before optional officers arrive.

## Decisions, clues and consequences

### 1985 opening — S01–S14

S01 establishes the volunteer, tower, cassette and letter in a compact opening. S03 allows second thoughts or the early failure E01. The train arrives at S02; Doc and the bracelet are introduced at S04.

S05's optional observation establishes someone else is moving. S07 offers interference with the clamp; S08 allows backing out or the fracture ending E02. S09 explains the three required parts, the interval damage below twenty minutes and the permanent lock at zero, and starts the visible countdown. Chasing Rex and searching his departure point through S10–S11 costs ten minutes and obtains his staff keycard.

At S13 Doc knows his safe combination and retrieves the regulator. The lab also establishes the setting catch that holds clock hands while gears run. There is no amnesia/date quiz and no S13x. S14 accounts for the first forty-five minutes of preparation, forecasts the remaining component work and final assembly, and lets the player choose era order.

### 1885 — S20–S29, S26b

The player chooses to go alone or take Doc's concealed escort. Calling at the blacksmith's window creates E03; keeping Doc concealed reaches the depot.

At the depot, stealth or a conversation leads to the same crate through different encounters. Protecting the stationmaster can succeed through a bluff, or lead to the saloon. A radio rescue is a recoverable fifteen-minute complication; offering the time train causes E04. After either successful bluff or rescue, the stationmaster thanks the player from his window and closes the shutter before the player reaches the crate alone.

The crate presents a certified, installed wheel and an untested boxed replacement whose packing slip requires fitting and regulation before use. Taking the latter preserves the installed clock's history. Doc tests it on return and calibrates it to the regulator; it works.

Taking the original reaches S25. Doc spots removal marks, explains the altered historical record despite a usable replacement, and sends the player back. S26b costs fifteen minutes and restores history before S26. Refusing to repair reaches E05 in the ravine, not in 1985.

S26 fits and calibrates the spare, accounts for seventy minutes of depot travel and preparation apart from complications, and departs for 2085 or, if the cell is already aboard, 1985.

### 2085 — S30–S38

The plaza offers the museum, shop, kiosk and (with the keycard) staff door. S30 discreetly establishes Tannen Temporal Security branding on the traffic drone before any police-report choice. Its twenty-minute cost covers finding a legal landing approach; S20's twenty covers navigating to the concealed ravine.

- S30k costs ten minutes: the player insists on finding a complaints procedure through promotional menus; the Temporal Authority handles unlicensed time travel and local security belongs to Tannen.
- S31 costs ten minutes: the player sees their possible failure, the falsification of residents' consent and Rex's live audience demonstration. This enables the final choice to ask Rex to introduce the player to his customers, without the choice label naming the broadcast mechanism.
- S33 requires the keycard. The jump log proves the clamp's origin. Exporting it to the Authority requires the kiosk knowledge; taking it to local police or stealing a rack cell leads to capture.
- S32–S35 offers a legitimate trade: the Walkman for a cell, keeping the personal cassette.
- S34/S36/S37 turn infiltration or theft into a recoverable capture/rescue, with a fifteen-minute penalty. S37 explicitly distinguishes that delay from the seventy-minute component expedition/preparation base. A bluff instead leads to E06.
- S38 provides a loaned cell, a warrant and the Authority's public-connection constraint. No automatic rescue is promised. All three cell-acquisition routes narrate the necessary matching of future power to the regulator as part of their seventy-minute base.

Every cell-acquisition route goes to 1885 if the wheel is missing, otherwise to S40. Era order changes context, not arbitrary extra travel.

### Finale — S40–S45

S40 returns to the same frozen moment with both parts. S41 spends seventy minutes assembling/calibrating the stabilizer together. If the countdown expires, E07 occurs in the train cab.

S42 places the player at the clock's setting catch while Doc connects the device and Rex intervenes. Three approaches:
1. Trade the stabilizer for escape: E08.
2. Turn Rex toward the people in the square and operate the catch: S43. The player saves Hill Valley; Rex escapes. E09 or E11.
3. With museum knowledge, ask Rex to introduce the player to his customers: S44. The player overcomes his reluctance, challenges his false consent claim on air and operates the catch. The broadcast sets `rexExposed`. Without the Authority he escapes under public scrutiny (E09/E11). With the Authority the live link allows arrest, setting `rexCaught`, then S45 and E10/E11.

The best route requires museum knowledge, the keycard/log, independent-authority knowledge, the actual broadcast decision and sufficient time. Alerting the Authority but choosing S43 is an ordinary rescue, not an unexplained arrest.
