# The Fratelli Run — second-pass editorial review

**Current status:** revised and independently passed. The body preserves the baseline critique; implementation and acceptance are recorded at the end.

**Full spoilers: routes, clues, reversals and all endings.**

Reviewed against `stories/fratelli-run/story.ts` at **8df3b37**, its parent, `story-outline.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and the engine's choice, effect and conditional-text semantics. I read all current prose, choices and ending variants. Routes below are source-traced; supplied measurements use actual engine-rendered Medium routes. I did not conduct a timed reader playtest or a browser playthrough. I inspected the installed T02 and P01 illustrations specifically for clue presentation; this is not a full artwork audit.

**Audience assumption:** confident readers roughly 10–14, families reading together, and adults returning to the franchise; no knowledge of the film required. “Official tie-in standard” below means the editorial standard I would commission against, not actual licensor approval or verified canon compliance.

## Commissioning verdict

**Revise and resubmit, with a targeted final developmental pass.** This is an engaging adventure with a publishable premise, unusually good atmosphere and a much stronger emotional conclusion than a routine franchise imitation. The previous pass made substantial improvements. It has not yet solved the distinction between an apparently considerate choice, a satisfying inference, and a hidden qualification for the best ending.

The outstanding problems are concentrated: the signal sequence teaches a lesson that the player cannot subsequently apply; the final evidence sacrifice can feel physically contrived; responsible adult help still leads to an explicitly bad outcome even when the reader supplies the exact location; and several important actions are skipped while their moral significance is explained at length. These need refinement, not another layer of systems, a longer story or additional lethal traps.

My likely first-reader response is: *I want to see what happens, I care about these friends, and the ending feels earned emotionally; I am less convinced that all the rules deciding which ending I deserve are fair.* That is an editorial forecast, not measured audience feedback.

## What now works, and should survive revision

**The hook is immediate and specific.** D01 combines a two-dollar private-eye costume, an escaped criminal family and an object hidden in an ordinary purchase. The key's `7 · LOW WATER` tag is a strong tactile mystery. “Twelve. With a toaster” and Mouth's reply give the premise comedy without puncturing it. The shorter opening is decisively better than the parent's long geographical and backstory introduction.

The protagonist is anonymous without being empty. “You still knock” at D01 says more about belonging than a paragraph of explanation. D03's outstretched hand, U04's imagined account of the crossing, and E11's hesitation while telling the story create a recognizable arc: wanting admission becomes participating, trusting and being listened to. The overcoat remains useful as costume, clue, hiding place and physical liability; E10's torn seam is an especially good payoff. Preserve these concrete links. Additional biographical detail or a named protagonist is unnecessary.

**The setting does real narrative work.** Rain, restricted sightlines, tide, street closures, Halloween costumes and fireworks shape decisions. U01's shrinking entrance, U06's pilings and P03's slowly descending wet line are effective cinematic writing. The public celebration offers safety and misunderstanding at once. The plot avoids repeating a pirate-treasure hunt while retaining secret routes, improvised equipment, boisterous criminals and children doing something consequential together.

The revised ensemble is functional. D04 assigns jobs; Mouth receives discoveries at D05; Data's light guides F01; Mikey reaches the sheriff; Chunk makes Sloth's return practical. They no longer simply wait for the viewpoint character to finish the adventure. E09's small lie about the fireworks and E10's shaking hands behind “Obviously” are particularly persuasive friendship writing. They also give ordinary victories emotional dignity.

**Sloth is substantially better written than in the parent.** D07 recognizes his accumulated knowledge; D08 gives him terms; U05 requires cooperation; P06 permits refusal; F01 makes companionship reciprocal. The removal of chocolate as an implied payment, abandonment underground, and automatic bodyguard behavior is valuable. E11's friends leaving him space is stronger than compulsory hoisting and applause-as-validation. Do not undo these repairs to regain pace or familiar iconography.

Newcomer provision is good overall. The Goonies, counterfeiting plates, Sloth's relationship to the Fratellis and the Lowline are explained when needed. The reader does not need to recognize an actor, quotation or famous prop to solve the plot. Some early information is administrative—auction lot, family relationship, two police names, shift time, tide time, signal protocol—but most earns its place. Shortening repeated explanations is preferable to removing essential context.

## Literary finish: atmosphere is ahead of dramatization

The narration has a confident dry wit: Mama ringing the bell while the children are already on the porch, Francis “inventing” a word, and Jake blaming Francis for mud. Mama's courteous possession language gives her a distinct menace. Her desire to recover both plates and Sloth connects property and control without requiring a sympathetic villain backstory. Her late arrival also supplies a useful escalation.

Mouth's vanity and concealed loyalty are clear; Data's gadget precision and Mikey's earnestness are recognizable. However, the revised emotional dialogue sometimes makes all the children sound like the same thoughtful adult. Keep their consideration, but express more of it through their individual habits: Mouth covers feeling with a joke, Data solves a practical problem, Mikey makes room. Chunk has become a capable caretaker; one brief joke, irritation or shared pleasure with Sloth would help them feel like friends as well as a support arrangement. Sloth needs no elaborate speech, but a preference or wry observation unrelated to danger would give him life beyond strength, fear and consent.

The prose often states the interpretation immediately after delivering the evidence. Examples include D07's “he has been solving his own,” U03's lesson about two wrong turns, P07's “doorstop,” F01's explanation that neither carries the other, and E11's statements about freedom and secrecy. Each is defensible alone; together they create a commentary track. The best concluding image—muddy shoes and friends listening—already communicates the arc. Trust it. Retain the spoken stop rule and practical safeguarding; trim the narrator's repeated certificates that the behavior is correct.

Conversely, some promised fun is absent. At U04 the reader chooses to fire a homemade launcher and swing over water. U05 begins “Beyond the sump”; the test, swing and landing never occur on the page. U05's teamwork likewise cuts directly to the outfall. These are signature adventure moments, not transit to summarize. Two or three arrival-dependent sentences can dramatize a hook taking weight, a sleeve snagging, or a shared lift without new pages. Trade existing explanation for action rather than expanding the run.

The familiar elements are mostly genre pleasures, not disqualifying clichés. The weaker ones are functional defaults: the convenient villain schedule overheard at P03; the crowd mistaking real trouble for a costume performance at F03; the universal instant recognition of printing plates by officials. Keep the Halloween misunderstanding, but let recognition evolve sooner once friends and the sheriff intervene. Give the brothers one immediate argument that naturally reveals their schedule rather than reciting it for the listener. These are small execution improvements, not reasons to abandon the premise.

## Choices, inference and reader trust

### A good destination is easy to find; the exact best route is less fairly legible

The main line generally rewards the visibly sensible option. At U02 river air identifies the river route; the map explicitly labels it. P01 places suspicious boots above and dangerous water below. P02's wording heavily favors “Back out while you still can.” P03 offers the strategically articulate “Keep the light covered until the footing is dry.” P05 contrasts leaving a frightened friend's decision to him with asking him to hold off his abusive family. These are accessible choices, but relatively few require the reader to connect separated clues.

T02 is the best existing inference test. A convincing claim to authority conflicts with the earlier van description and the immediately preceding dispatch information. Refusing verbally causes a recoverable chase; escaping through the library avoids it. The revision rightly removed the narrator's announcement that the moustache is fake. The installed picture shows the blue-striped van and a suspicious-looking officer without an obvious peeling disguise; it supports the clue rather than simply announcing the answer.

The signal sequence is less successful. D04 teaches the protocol; D06 rehearses it; P03 explicitly supplies waiting for dry footing as an option; P06 then offers the entire correct procedure, “Send two low flashes ... wait for one back.” This is a coordination decision, but not a difficult act of remembered inference. F03 then explains that the prepared gap is available and offers the winning handoff only if every condition already passes. The last click is confirmation, not the decisive insight.

Conditional affordances are not inherently a fault: offering a launcher only when the player has one is correct. Nor should a blocked action be displayed merely to manufacture a trap. The issue is that the manuscript claims a best outcome requiring independently connected clues, while its key choices supply the operational answer and its interface suppresses a plausible attempt for some readers. Strengthen one real inference in this existing chain rather than making every menu obscure. Establish the environmental relationship earlier, keep the relevant actions neutrally phrased, and let the reader choose timing for a reason they can articulate.

### The premature signal is only partly recoverable

P04 is a useful setback: the player receives an answer, alerts Francis and realizes the water prevents useful movement. It costs ten margin. But its closing thought—“Now you have to give them something they can do”—promises adaptation. P06 never permits an early-signaling reader to signal again, because only `signal=ready` qualifies. That is not explained by a broken flashlight, withdrawn allies or a permanently blocked sightline. The friends are still available.

Thus the mistake permits ordinary success but permanently removes E11, independently of the remaining margin. An early-signal prepared Medium route reaches E10 with 30 remaining. A wrong fork costing fifteen can still reach E11 with 20. The difference may be intentional, but the story does not make the irreversible consequence legible. The smaller repair is to allow a later corrective signal, with an appropriate already-incurred cost and changed response, rather than suggest that learning is possible and then hide the action. If the author wants this to be an irreversible coordination failure, the scene must establish why.

### The final bottleneck needs a believable physical cause

F03's unqualified arrival says the group can get the child safe but cannot pass “two sharp-edged plates” through the crowd. That explanation applies even when the light was correctly timed and there is ample margin, if Sloth is absent. P07 puts the plates inside the overcoat; F03 then narrates raising them overhead, matching its illustration. The defined props are small banknote-sized matrices, and the reader has not chosen that exposed display. A gap admitting the child but requiring the plates to be thrown into a fire feels like the desired ending enforcing its own physics.

The prepared group and Sloth's moment of resistance can legitimately produce a richer climax. A freely chosen companion unlocking a distinctive ensemble ending is not, by itself, treating him as equipment. The concern is the generic failure explanation concealing several different causes, plus a menu where the tactful choice always produces continued accompaniment. Show the actual consequence of the missing preparation: a delayed opening, lost sightline, interrupted handoff or pursuer reaching the coat. If Sloth's intervention is decisive, make that specific difference visible. Do not merely add more moral approval around his presence.

P05 would also benefit from a viable, respectful return-home option, rather than making departure occur only after assigning him the dangerous rear guard. It need not deliver an identical celebration or erase the value of choosing to stay. The acceptance test is that respecting a boundary feels like a legitimate relationship choice, not an indirect route through an obviously exploitative request.

### Adult help remains a structural problem

D02/T04/E03 are much kinder and more credible than the parent's dismissal of children as pranksters. Nevertheless, “Leave the key with him as evidence” still leads to a **bad** ending, including after the library has identified the exact ice house and the player has shown the chart to Olafsen. E03 says “You were right to ask for help,” while the outcome system says the adventure failed. A bad criminal outcome need not imply bad moral conduct; the concern is the cumulative pattern and the paucity of a meaningful next action. The reader cannot ensure the precise report reaches the responsible officer or accept a smaller legitimate handover victory.

D02 also takes ten margin, enough to remove clean-best eligibility on Hard, while its report has no later practical advantage. Mouth says the record counts; the mechanics and later events give it little weight. This teaches a more cynical strategy than the prose intends.

Preserve adults' limitations and the child's decisive role. A modest distinction between an unexplained key deposit and an informed, location-specific report would help: the latter might produce a legitimate limited success or a recovery route, while an earlier logged report could make one later verification easier. This is not a request to make police omnipotent or every careful choice optimal. It is a request that supplying useful evidence materially differs from putting a mystery object in a drawer.

### There are enough pitfalls

Seven bad endings are ample. E02 has fair evidence; E04 follows a clear, serious environmental risk; E05 follows approaching a watched access point; E07 honors Mama's literal promise but exposes its limits. E01 is a knowingly premature opt-out. They do not need more punishment added. E04's rescue and dry socks are particularly well pitched: danger has consequences without humiliation or graphic harm.

The least persuasive failure mechanism is accumulated margin exhaustion, where many kinds of delay converge on E06's retrospective capture. The revision avoids location teleportation, but sacrifices immediacy. A short scene-specific search detail at the point of exhaustion would do more for fairness than lengthening E06. Readers should recognize *how this exit closed*, not only that a number reached zero.

Branching is broad early and convergent late. That is suitable for this length; shared climaxes do not invalidate agency. P08's escape, preservation, destruction and bargain alternatives are materially different. Conversely, several subchoices are one-way improvements: the launcher eventually repays its small preparation cost, and the shelf simply beats wading on margin and comfort. These can be ordinary adventure decisions, but they should not all be described as strategic trade-offs.

Replay value is **moderate, not eleven wholly different adventures**. The library encounter, hideout chase, street approach, Sloth scenes and two escape directions provide real new reading. Most bad endings vary how the same criminal objective succeeds, often followed by counterfeit notes circulating. Once the preparation flags are understood, replay is chiefly efficient routing through familiar scenes. Fresh prose for a handful of already-distinct action outcomes would improve this more than more ending labels.

## Ranked revision brief

### Must resolve before publication

1. **Make the signal setback and later choice agree — P03–P06.** The lesson currently promises recovery while the action disappears. Smallest remedy: permit a causally credible corrective signal, or dramatize the irreversible loss of that possibility. **Acceptance:** a reader can explain why the later action is possible or impossible from events, without knowing a flag value; a nominal ten-point error does not conceal an unexplained permanent best lockout.

2. **Make the climax's outcome differences physical and specific — P07, F01, F03, E10/E11.** Replace the generic sharp-plates bottleneck with the actual consequence of missing preparation, position or coordinated support. Keep safety over property and Sloth's voluntary contribution. **Acceptance:** a reader with a prepared signal and ample margin can understand their result, including when Sloth is absent; small evidence the protagonist has carried successfully does not become inexplicably impossible to carry through a child-sized gap.

3. **Give informed adult help a proportionate consequence — D02, T01, T04, E03.** Preserve the new respectful adults. Differentiate a recorded, location-specific warning from the bare key deposit; let a report achieve something useful or permit recovery. **Acceptance:** readers are not told their detailed report matters while seeing exactly the same failure as an uninformed handover; Hard optimization does not simply teach “skip the responsible phone call.”

### High-value polish

4. **Put the adventure on the page and reduce its explanation — U04–U06; D07–D08; P07; E11.** Spend a few saved sentences on the chosen crossing/lift and a character-specific human beat. Add a respectful return-home alternative at P05 if departure remains a relational choice. **Acceptance:** major selected actions have a visible consequence; Sloth can express a preference beyond helping or refusing abuse; the narrator does not immediately paraphrase every emotional achievement.

5. **Sharpen one remembered inference — D04/D06, P03/P06, F03.** Keep useful knowledge-gated options, but stop supplying every operational step and success explanation at the decision itself. **Acceptance:** an attentive first-time reader can cite the earlier evidence behind the best coordination choice, while a missed connection creates an understandable setback rather than arbitrary guessing.

6. **Trim repeated setup and close the remaining human stakes — D05, E08.** Compress return-only hub text; retain new reports. Explicitly connect E08's report to Sloth's protection. Check P01's image/prose relationship: the installed image clearly reveals the iron door even when no-map prose treats its location as undiscovered. **Acceptance:** repeat arrivals advance the situation, ordinary victory resolves the announced personal threat, and art does not inadvertently answer a concealed discovery.

### Optional taste, not publication blockers

A little more irreverent ensemble banter, a less stock crowd-misunderstanding beat, and one concrete local consequence of counterfeit money could enrich the franchise texture. They should replace weaker lines, not become new quests. Do not add pirates, famous catchphrases, a second MacGuffin or harsher failures to make the adventure seem bigger.

The final editorial check should be a small first-reader session, including a newcomer and a reader who signals early or reports the chart to police. Ask what they thought would happen, what evidence informed that expectation, and whether the resulting ending felt understandable. That will test the remaining publication question more directly than another structural pass: **does the reader experience the story's consequences as earned?**
